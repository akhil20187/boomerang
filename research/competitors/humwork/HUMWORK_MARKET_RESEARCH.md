# Humwork: services, activity, funding and demos

Research snapshot: **3 October 2026**. This combines public-source research with the supplied [57-screen audit](../../../humwork-audit/user-journey.md) and [screenshot analysis](HUMWORK_SCREENSHOT_RESEARCH.md). No paid expert consultation or external integration was tested.

## My assessment

**Humwork is a direct competitor to your expert-second-opinion idea.** Its public product is built around an agent passing a problem and context to a human, then receiving help inside the same workflow. Your urgent store-calling use case has less visible overlap: the evidence here shows expert chat, rather than a worker calling local shops and returning verified inventory.

There is evidence of a functioning product and a real launch. There is insufficient evidence to judge present consultation volume, service quality, revenue or business momentum. Sparse retrieved social activity does not establish that the company has stopped operating.

## How the handoff works

Humwork's public [MCP repository](https://github.com/humworkai/humwork-mcp) documents starting an expert consultation with the problem, previous attempts and context, exchanging messages, closing the session and rating it. MCP is the connection standard through which an agent calls the service.

The documentation includes Claude Code, Claude web, Cursor, Codex CLI, ChatGPT, Windsurf, Lovable and OpenClaw. These are documented integration paths, not integrations tested in this research. Hermes was not explicitly documented in the inspected material.

This overlaps two of your proposed benefits: avoiding repeated explanations and returning the human's response to the agent. The harder distinction is the job performed and the quality of the result.

The [public GitHub organisation](https://github.com/humworkai) showed the integration repository last updated **11 June 2026**. Its inspected repository page showed 28 commits, one star and one fork. These small public numbers do not measure customers: the hosted service can change without updates to its public connector.

## Pricing and operating model

The [screenshot audit](HUMWORK_SCREENSHOT_RESEARCH.md) captured Starter at **$30/month for 30 credits** and Pro at **$100/month for 110 credits**. One credit represents approximately $1 of balance value; it is not a universal per-minute rate. The Indian checkout's ₹2,999.34 monthly amount was capture-time currency conversion, not a fixed India price. No payment was submitted.

Their [terms, updated 8 April 2026](https://humwork.ai/terms), specify time-based billing in prepaid increments, a minimum consultation charge and a disclosed expert rate. Unused parts of billed increments are not refunded. Credit purchases generally are final. Experts require assessment, administrative approval and payout setup. A platform service fee exists, but its percentage was not disclosed in the inspected terms.

The terms also limit the status of professional advice. Advertising legal or financial domains does not establish a regulated advisory engagement, suitable credentials or a usable second opinion for a particular customer.

## Comparison with your direction

| Dimension | Humwork evidence | Your proposed first use case |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Agent needs specialist help | Customer needs urgent local information; agent hands off calls |
| Worker | Domain expert in a chat | Human who calls stores and gathers answers |
| Output | Expert response returned to agent | Store-by-store model, colour, stock, final price and offers |
| Context | Problem and previous attempts transferred | Shopping requirements transferred without repetition |
| Charging | Credits, time increments, minimum charge | Proposed connected-minute charge, small unanswered-call fee and task cap |
| Quality question | Did expert help resolve the problem? | Were stock and price answers current, complete and attributable to stores? |

Your broad expert-help idea already has direct competition. The urgent local-calling task offers a more concrete difference in the evidence reviewed. It is a possible starting point, not a claim of uniqueness or proof of demand.

**Next step:** test one actual agent-to-human store-calling handoff and record the returned information and total cost. That tests the operating difference identified here; it does not make a 15-minute promise your value proposition.
