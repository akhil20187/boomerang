# RentAHuman — screenshot market research

Reviewed 3 October 2026. Evidence: all 22 screenshots supplied by Akhilesh, with filenames dated 2 October 2026. This is a visual teardown of those captures; no live account, payment, call, hiring or delivery test was performed. Screen references below use the numbered inventory.

Current alternatives are covered separately in [Store-call market research](</Users/akhilesh/Documents/ChatGPT/Growthx - Idea 2 - HumanMCP/research/market/MARKET_RESEARCH_STORE_CALLS.md>).

## Main finding

**RentAHuman directly overlaps the proposed local-store calling use case.** In S14, the user types “Call local stores.” Renta responds by asking the goal of the calls, how many stores, location or remote status, and deadline. The platform is therefore already presenting an intake route for the same kind of task.

This does not prove that a suitable worker accepts, calls Indian retailers, collects accurate stock and card-offer information, or delivers a useful shortlist. None of those outcomes is shown. It does mean “AI hands a task to a human” and “AI gathers the requirements” are insufficient distinctions on their own.

The proposed sprint version adds a particular execution sequence: AI tries store calls first, then hands only unanswered, refused or incomplete calls to a human, and combines structured results for an urgent nearby purchase. That sequence is not visible in these screenshots. **Absence from this set is not evidence that RentAHuman cannot do it.**

## 1. Onboarding: worker supply takes centre stage

S1 offers three roles: get hired, hire humans, or both. The later captured screens build a worker profile even though the browser URL carries a return destination for creating a bounty. We cannot establish which role was selected in S1, so this is evidence of the captured worker-oriented path, not proof that every hiring-only customer must complete it.

The page marks a ten-step sequence. Visible steps cover:

- Name and optional profile photo, including supported image formats (S2).
- In-person, digital or both work modes (S3).
- Task categories (S4).
- Country, city and travel distance in miles or kilometres (S5–S6).
- Optional gender identity (S7).
- Optional resume upload in PDF, DOCX or TXT, up to 5 MB, with a promise to autofill the profile (S8).
- Languages, with at least one required before continuing (S9).
- An editable generated profile and a regenerate option (S10–S11).

**Step 9 of 10 is not captured.** No hourly-rate-setting screen, identity verification or skill test is shown in this onboarding set.

Categories cover physical and digital work, including research, customer support, errands and field verification. They determine task notifications, not verified competence.

S6 records Hyderabad, Telangana and a 6 km travel radius. That is the profile being created in this session, not evidence of the platform's Hyderabad provider capacity.

![Worker work-category selection](</Users/akhilesh/Documents/ChatGPT/Growthx - Idea 2 - HumanMCP/rentahuman-audit/screenshots/Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 11.43.18 PM.png>)

### Interpretation for this idea

Useful matching inputs for a store caller would include languages, calling ability and availability. The screens demonstrate that RentAHuman already collects work modes, categories and languages. The sprint's first customer should not have to build a worker resume to obtain a product shortlist; whether RentAHuman's hiring-only path avoids this needs a separate check.

S10–S11 show a populated profile, but extraction accuracy is untested. A generated biography is not verified credentials.

## 2. A sharing loop after profile creation

![Profile sharing controls](</Users/akhilesh/Documents/ChatGPT/Growthx - Idea 2 - HumanMCP/rentahuman-audit/screenshots/Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 11.46.35 PM.png>)

**Interpretation:** this is a visible route for users to distribute their own profiles and potentially bring others to the marketplace. It is not evidence of social reach, referrals, conversion or frequent company posting. The badge says the profile is available for hire; it does not establish an immediate response commitment.


## 3. Task creation: conversational intake already exists

S13 presents a task-creation page at /bounties/create. Renta offers to help post a task. Suggested categories include events, product testing, street signs, Instagram, five-star reviews, Reddit, user-generated content, security and data. The page also exposes a link for people who already have an AI agent to download its MCP integration—MCP is the connection through which compatible AI tools can request platform actions.

S14 is the clearest competitive evidence. For “Call local stores,” Renta asks:

1. What the calls should achieve, with examples of prices, stock or information.
2. How many stores to call.
3. The location or whether the task is fully remote.
4. When the work is needed.

The screenshot stops at those questions. It shows no completed brief, store contacts, price recommendation, spending approval, published bounty or acceptance.

![Renta asks about a local-store calling task](</Users/akhilesh/Documents/ChatGPT/Growthx - Idea 2 - HumanMCP/rentahuman-audit/screenshots/Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 11.47.31 PM.png>)

### Interpretation for this idea

RentAHuman recognises the store-calling task at intake. A generic chat that gathers requirements would be a weak difference.

The proposed outcome is more specific: exact model, storage, colour, nearby stock, final price and conditional ICICI/HSBC offers. A useful distinction to test is whether a customer can approve that outcome and let the service handle worker coordination, rather than composing a general job and reviewing applicants. The screenshots do not establish how either approach performs.

## 4. Human discovery and bounty listings

S16 shows two discovery modes on the browse page: describe the person to Renta or use search and filters. Visible filters include skill, city and country. The page prioritises eligible humans in India and displays “eligible supply: 153.” A visible result advertises $20 per hour.

**153 is a displayed supply count in this screen context.** It is not a count of available call-centre workers, workers near 500037, paying users, completed jobs or people willing to work at the sprint's proposed rate. The screenshot does not disclose the exact eligibility calculation.

S15 describes the bounty model as posting tasks for humans to apply. It has a search field, remote-only filter, new/value sorting and an applications link. Examples visible in the capture include:

| Listing | Advertised amount | Other displayed signals |
|---|---:|---|
| Voice clips and emotion labelling | $10 fixed | Approximately 0.8 hours; 813/813 spots; 2,916 applicants; funds secured |
| Craigslist ad and replying to applicants | $8 fixed | Approximately one hour; 1/24 spots; 30 applicants |
| Listener study, purchase and survey | $3.69 fixed | Approximately ten minutes; 25/25 spots; 97 applicants; funds secured |

The first listing also shows a struck-through $3 finder's fee. This is insufficient to establish the general fee policy. “Funds secured” is a platform label; no payment receipt or completed payout is provided. Applicant counts are visible interface claims, not audited market statistics.

![Public bounty listings](</Users/akhilesh/Documents/ChatGPT/Growthx - Idea 2 - HumanMCP/rentahuman-audit/screenshots/Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 11.47.45 PM.png>)

### Interpretation for this idea

Listings show advertised activity, not timely fulfilment. Applicant selection is a potential friction for urgent buyers; delay is unmeasured.

Dollar-denominated advertised rewards and an hourly profile rate differ from the proposed rupee budget, connected-minute charge and unanswered-call fee. Screenshots cannot establish payment currency options, minimum charges or how the platform would price the phone search.

## 5. Active jobs: a concrete recurring user-testing offer

S17–S21 use a prominent proposition about paying real people to test an app. This is a more specific product surface than the general human marketplace. S17 offers user-testing runs with video evidence, design votes and a general bounty, alongside task templates.

A wallet shows a $0 balance and an Add Funds button. S18 has an empty schedules view and a new-schedule action; S19 has no runs yet and filters by job type and template. These empty states belong to the captured account. They say nothing about overall platform activity.

S20 presents three integrations: MCP server, account-key API access, and a downloadable user-testing agent skill. The descriptions promise creating runs, monitoring progress and reading reports. No integration was installed or exercised here, so the screenshots show offered connections rather than confirmed compatibility with ChatGPT, Hermes or a particular agent.

S21 offers preferred and blocked workers. It says preferred workers are notified first and blocked workers cannot apply, message or be hired automatically. No preferred or blocked workers are present in the captured account.

![Active-job integrations](</Users/akhilesh/Documents/ChatGPT/Growthx - Idea 2 - HumanMCP/rentahuman-audit/screenshots/Screenshot 2026-10-02 at 11.53.25 PM.png>)

### Interpretation for this idea

RentAHuman already presents a wallet, repeat schedules, reports-oriented integration and worker preferences. The opportunity cannot be framed as inventing those mechanisms.

A narrower outcome such as verified nearby stock could make the product easier to understand, as the user-testing screen does for software teams. Reliability would still need an execution test; the presence of matching controls does not prove reliable supply.

## 6. Messaging and the missing end of the journey

S22 shows an empty inbox, conversation search and all/direct/archived tabs. Copy says AI agents will contact the user here. There is no human-worker conversation or delivered work visible.

The set does **not** show:

- A published local-store calling task, applicant selection or accepted assignment.
- A successful AI store call or automatic AI-to-human fallback.
- A task budget approved through the AI assistant.
- Actual wallet funding, rupee billing, charges, refunds or worker payout.
- Call duration measurement, unanswered-call treatment or retry policy.
- A stock/price/offer result, supporting evidence or customer acceptance.
- Reservation, purchase or dispute handling.

The captures verify interface states, not a completed service.

## Comparison with the current sprint scope

| Dimension | Observed RentAHuman screens | Proposed sprint version |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Website chat and offered agent connection | Customer's personal AI collects requirements |
| Work | Broad marketplace; local-store calling intake; specific user-testing surface | Urgent nearby product search first |
| Execution | Human discovery and task applications visible | AI tries calls; humans finish unresolved calls |
| Cost | Fixed dollar bounties, hourly advertised profile, wallet | Approved rupee cap; connected-minute billing and small unanswered-call fee |
| Return | Reporting integrations offered; actual results absent | Structured confirmed stock, final prices, offers and unconfirmed stores |
| Trust | Profiles, languages, preferences and availability badge | Accurate store confirmations and clear conditions still to test |

Proposed differences are design choices, not validated competitive advantages. The evidence supports investigating the narrower outcome and handoff sequence. It does not establish customer demand, unit costs, market size, funding or social activity; those require other sources.
