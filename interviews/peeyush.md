# Interview with Peeyush

- Date: 2 October 2026
- Topic: His senior hiring process, feedback on referral-based hiring, and feedback on an AI workflow that hands unfinished tasks or requests for expert review to humans.
- Transcript note: The supplied transcript labels the entire conversation “Me,” includes unrelated opening audio, and uses both Peeyush and Piyush in the wording. Speaker labels and wording are preserved below. The takeaways attribute statements using the conversational context; they are not a corrected speaker transcript.
- Evidence status: Reported experience, opinions and a conditional offer to help demonstrate an integration. No paid pilot, named end user, delivery date or price was agreed.

## Takeaways

### Main finding

Peeyush already fills many startup roles through trusted connections and his IIIT Bangalore alumni group. He explicitly said application volume is not currently a challenge because he is not hiring in bulk. This supports the value he places on personal recommendations, but does not establish an unmet need for another referral platform.

He preferred the alternative idea of handing AI work to a human when AI cannot finish a task or when the user wants an expert second opinion. His strongest advice was to choose one concrete problem that people can understand and complete within two weeks. He offered to explore an integration or demonstration through InvestValue, but the call did not establish recurring demand, willingness to pay or a committed pilot.

### His reported hiring process

1. Search LinkedIn using domain or skill keywords and, where useful, company or other profile criteria.
2. Look for second-degree connections so someone he knows can comment on the candidate's effectiveness.
3. Shortlist candidates and ask recruitment or HR to conduct initial screening and arrange discussions.
4. Depending on the mutual connection, reach out separately for feedback.
5. For Naukri, give the recruitment team search criteria and a screening questionnaire, including the kinds of responses expected.
6. More recently, use normal LinkedIn posts and his personal network rather than relying only on formal job postings. Responses can arrive through WhatsApp, calls or email.
7. His most common current route is posting requirements in his IIIT Bangalore alumni group. He described this as his highest-hit-rate channel, without supplying measured conversion figures.
8. For recommended candidates, review the profile, ask them to read the company website, then arrange HR or technical discussions if they remain interested.

He said he has not done bulk hiring during roughly the last three to four years of building his startup. The exact period and hiring volume were not established.

### What he values in a recommendation

- Whether someone is personally vouching for the candidate or merely forwarding a resume. He explicitly asks which it is.
- A source he knows who can comment on the candidate's effectiveness.
- Suitability for startup work, where he considers personal connections useful for commitment to the organisation.
- References generally take precedence over direct job applications in his current process.

These are his stated preferences. They do not establish that references predict performance or retention better than other evidence.

### Pain points and concerns he described

- A referee may find an additional interview burdensome unless it is easy or sufficiently motivating.
- Knowing a candidate personally does not necessarily mean the referee can assess the technical or domain expertise required for the role.
- A reward could encourage people to submit random resumes rather than candidates whose work they know.
- Answers could be generated with AI or invented. Capturing a relationship does not by itself establish that the testimony is true.
- He did not see a strong enough distinction between the proposed referral platform and existing products.
- A new marketplace needs employers to supply openings and candidates or referees to participate. He questioned why an employer would post there before the platform has a user base.
- Distribution and differentiation were larger concerns for him than whether hiring is a broadly important problem.

Repeated interviews for a referee recommending several people were raised by Akhilesh. Peeyush said the burden was plausible and needed testing; he did not report a specific observed case.

| Proposal | His reaction | What remains untested |

His comments about existing competitors are anecdotal and unverified. No competitor product was inspected during this call.

### Alternative idea discussed — human handoff for AI work

Akhilesh called this “human MCP”: a connection through which an AI system can request work or review from a human and receive the result back.

Two distinct mechanisms were proposed:

1. **Finish an incomplete task:** an AI calls stores for stock and price information, then gives unsuccessful calls to a human who finishes them and returns the results.
2. **Get expert review:** AI gathers context and does research, then passes the context and unresolved questions to a professional for a second opinion.

Other examples included financial planning, insurance, tax, school selection and offline coordination. A broad marketplace of providers, task charges and a prepaid balance were proposed. These were illustrative possibilities, not agreed scope, verified capabilities or validated prices.

### Feedback on the human handoff idea

| Proposal | His reaction | What remains untested |
|---|---|---|

He initially misunderstood Akhilesh as organising the build event. After learning that Akhilesh was participating, he strongly recommended the second idea, partly because he expected it to draw more interest and suit Akhilesh's presentation ability. That is an opinion about appeal, not proof of demand or judging outcomes.

### Implications for the ideas — interpretation

- Peeyush's current hiring workaround appears to serve him reasonably well. His preference for referrals does not make him a confirmed customer for the referral product.
- The distinction between forwarding a resume and personally vouching for someone is a useful question to preserve in reference collection.
- A referee's direct observations and ability to assess a particular kind of work are separate. The product should not treat familiarity as expertise.
- The hiring idea needs evidence of a problem remaining after trusted-network sourcing, not just evidence that referrals are preferred.
- The human handoff idea contains two different jobs: completing failed execution and reviewing an uncertain answer. The first test should choose one.
- Peeyush's conditional access offer is a possible way to shorten validation. It is not permission to use his systems or a booked provider.
- Novelty and presentation appeal should not replace a completed job for a real user.
- An end result that depends on Akhilesh manually delivering the service would test a different operating model from a product that works without him.
- This interview does not lock an idea or change the current product scope.

### Missing evidence

- A hiring failure or delay Peeyush currently needs help with.
- A named active opening and willingness to test the referral workflow.
- The identity and capabilities of the competing referral company he mentioned.
- Referee participation, candour and domain competence in a real interaction.
- One recurring task that AI cannot finish or that a user wants reviewed by a human.
- A named user who already spends time or money solving that task.
- A named human provider and an agreed response time.
- Specific integration access and a commitment from InvestValue.
- End-to-end completion, quality, cost and willingness to pay.
- Whether either idea can produce its promised result within the sprint's hours.
