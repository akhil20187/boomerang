# Interview with Yogi

- Date: 2 October 2026
- LinkedIn: https://www.linkedin.com/in/yogi100x/
- Topic: His hiring process, assessment project, and feedback on referral-based hiring.
- Transcript note: The supplied transcript labels all turns “Me.” Speaker labels are preserved as provided.

## Takeaways

### Main finding

Yogi reports low trust in resumes and uses practical work plus conversations to assess candidates. His spreadsheet already handles the initial application volume. The call does not establish that a referral platform would improve his hiring outcomes or that he would use or pay for it.

### His reported hiring process

1. Ask 4–5 trusted people in the relevant field for candidates.
2. If that does not produce candidates, post on LinkedIn, X and WhatsApp statuses.
3. Collect applications: approximately 300–350 for experienced roles in India. Fresher campaigns through his network of roughly 400 colleges can yield 2,000–2,400 applications. These are his reported figures, not independently verified counts.
4. Extract resume information into a Google Sheet, previously a Clay table, and mark whether candidates meet each role requirement.
5. Filter the requirement columns and usually speak with 5–6 candidates.
6. Give a take-home assignment, then discuss the submission, approach and use of AI.
7. Progress candidates based on their work and ability to explain it. Open resumes selectively when a claim needs checking.

He also described an earlier workflow where assessments preceded conversations. The current sequence needs clarification.

### Pain points he described

- Resume claims can be exaggerated or optimised to pass screening. He has taught candidates how to optimise resumes for applicant tracking systems, which contributes to his scepticism.
- Applicant tracking system match scores can be hard to understand: he is sometimes unsure why a candidate receives a particular score.
- Application volume makes manual review impractical. His spreadsheet reduces that work but still filters based on resume claims.
- Scheduled reference calls are unreliable. He estimated that 7 out of 10 are not answered on time; this is anecdotal, not a measured completion rate.
- Referees may resist spending 5–10 minutes speaking with another AI system.
- Candidates can coach friendly referees to repeat a prepared account of their work.

### Assessment project he described

Yogi built personalised assessments covering resume claims, multiple-choice questions, coding tasks, scenarios, portfolio evaluation and proctoring. He reports running two internship fairs involving approximately 15 companies and 800 students. This indicates reported use, but does not establish predictive accuracy or improved hiring outcomes. He is handing the project to a friend because he could not give it enough attention. He also described a broader system split across three platforms, including college placement management.

### Feedback on the referral idea

| Proposal | His reaction | What remains untested |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive reference questions and deeper follow-ups | Thought these could uncover useful detail. | Detailed answers can still be coached or false. |
| A link the referee opens whenever convenient | Accepted this as an answer to scheduled-call difficulty. | Actual participation, completion and time required. |
| Overlapping LinkedIn histories | Challenged whether this establishes honesty. | Shared employment dates do not prove direct collaboration or truthful testimony. |
| A bounty after a successful hire | Said the incentive made sense. | Whether it increases participation, what it costs, and whether it encourages exaggeration. |
| Blocking dishonest referees | Suggested blacklisting. | How false claims would be established, challenged and corrected. |

### Implications for the idea — interpretation

- Trusted-network sourcing and candidate-selected references create different kinds of trust. Yogi asks people he already trusts to suggest candidates; the proposed platform lets candidates nominate supporters.
- The product must add useful evidence beyond his existing spreadsheet, assignment and interview workflow. Reducing application volume alone is not established as the remaining unmet need.
- An AI conversation may improve specificity and organisation. It does not establish truthfulness.
- A reward tied to hiring may increase willingness to participate while also creating an incentive to praise the candidate. Test participation and evidence quality separately.
- His positive closing comments are encouragement, not a pilot or purchase commitment. No role, candidate, pilot date or price was agreed.

### Missing evidence

- The most costly failure or delay after his spreadsheet produces a shortlist.
- Whether capable candidates were excluded, and how he would know.
- Whether a substantive reference changes his interview list or clarifies a hiring decision.
- Referee completion without scheduled calls or a bounty.
- A named active senior role and a commitment to test the workflow.
- Willingness to pay for a completed reference or repeat the process.
