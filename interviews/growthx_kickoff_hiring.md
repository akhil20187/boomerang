## Takeaways

### Main finding

Participants described different points in the hiring process: reducing a large applicant pool, assessing an already-filtered shortlist, and using conversations or projects to judge initiative and working style. The discussion supports investigating what resume screening misses. It does not establish that a mandatory reference improves shortlisting, that good candidates were excluded, or that employers will use or pay for the proposed platform.

### Pain points and limitations they described

- Vaibhav needed to narrow a large resume pool and had already used Claude to do that.
- Akash only saw a small shortlist; he could not speak to the initial filtering process.
- Paresh wanted initiative, learning attitude and breadth beyond a candidate's current skills.
- Mohit wanted to learn things about working style and interests that a resume alone does not reveal.
- Akash questioned whether money is the right incentive for a senior referee or founder. He suggested status or reputation might matter more. This was a concern, not a tested result.
- The group did not establish how much interview time these gaps consume, the cost of a bad shortlist, or how often capable people are screened out.

### Akhilesh's perspective and proposal

Akhilesh described his startup background and his own experience of being rejected during HR screening. He asked how hiring managers know whether strong candidates were excluded before reaching them. This is his reported experience and concern; the discussion did not measure false rejections.

His proposal during the call:

1. An employer posts a role.
2. A candidate needs a substantive recommendation before the application reaches the hiring manager.
3. A referee who knows the candidate completes an AI-led interview rather than submitting a one-line endorsement.
4. The employer receives the candidate and the reference information.
5. Referee identity, relationship, seniority and possibly LinkedIn presence could influence weighting.
6. The employer could offer a bounty, with $100 mentioned as an illustrative amount.
7. The proposed focus is senior hires.

These are proposed mechanics, not validated requirements or approved scope. The amount, reward conditions, AI weighting and credibility checks remain unresolved. One sentence about who earns money is ambiguous in the transcript and should not be treated as an agreed payment model.

### Implications for the idea — interpretation

- The group described related needs, but not a single shared hiring workflow. The first product must choose a specific user and point in that workflow.
- Vaibhav is a possible research contact for initial shortlisting; Akash's experience is more relevant to interviewing an existing shortlist. Neither is a confirmed pilot user.
- Paresh's interest in initiative fits the proposed reference questions, but his lack of an active opening limits an immediate hiring test.
- Candidate-selected references and recommendations from a person the employer already trusts are different sources of confidence. The platform must make that difference visible.
- Asking for concrete firsthand examples may improve specificity. It does not establish truthfulness.
- Making a reference mandatory could exclude capable candidates with limited access to referees. The call did not establish that this trade-off improves the applicant pool.
- Referee seniority and social following should be recorded as context, not treated as proven measures of honesty or candidate quality.
- Hobbies and shared interests may start a conversation; they are not established evidence of job performance.
- Encouragement is not demand validation. No active role, candidate, pilot date or price was committed.

### Missing evidence

- A named hiring owner with an active senior opening and agreement to test.
- The specific costly decision or delay the reference should improve.
- A real candidate and a referee with direct experience of their work.
- Whether referees complete the process without a bounty.
- Whether the reference adds evidence beyond resumes, projects and interviews.
- Whether that evidence changes or clarifies a hiring manager's view.
- Employer willingness to repeat the process and pay.
- Whether a mandatory-reference gate excludes strong candidates.
- Referee participation and evidence quality with versus without rewards.
