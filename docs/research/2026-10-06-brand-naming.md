## Naming practices worth using

1. **Choose the job and audience first.** A name for a human-help network has different requirements from a discount-shopping app. Our name must survive expansion beyond stock checks.
2. **Prefer a distinctive name to a literal category description.** USPTO distinguishes suggestive, arbitrary and invented marks from weaker descriptive or generic terms. This is a useful naming principle, not a finding that any candidate is registrable in India. [USPTO: strong trademarks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks).
3. **Make it easy to read, say, spell and remember.** WIPO recommends checking these practical qualities and unwanted meanings in other languages. Shortness helps only when it preserves clarity. [WIPO: Making a Mark](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/wipo-pub-900-1-en-making-a-mark-an-introduction-to-trademarks-for-small-and-medium-sized-enterprises.pdf).
4. **Separate name from explanation.** The brand can be broad; “We call shops for stock, prices and offers” explains the first service. A name need not contain AI, human, calls or MCP.
5. **Test with people rather than trusting our taste.** Proposed test: say a candidate once, ask someone to type it; later ask what they remember and what service they expected. Use intended customers, including speakers of the languages we expect to serve. This test is proposed and has not been run.
6. **Check conflicts before investing in a logo.** Search exact names, similar spellings and sounds, related services, domains, app stores and handles. WIPO recommends checking national and regional registers alongside its global database. [WIPO search guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database), [India trademark public search](https://tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch/).
7. **Treat domain ownership as one input.** It saves purchase and setup work; it does not prove naming quality, trademark rights or that repurposing the site is sensible.

These are the main actionable practices, not an exhaustive account of every naming theory.

### Hello Aryan: candid assessment

Its strongest feature is the human contact story, not merely the domain. It can support a team rather than a single person and is broad enough for more than shopping. The current public description should still say that AI makes the calls, with human follow-up coming soon.

There is also a global naming concern: “Aryan” has documented Nazi racial associations. That does not invalidate someone's personal name; my inference is that an international standalone brand could create unwanted associations that an India-first audience may read differently. Test this with intended users before making it a global brand. [US Holocaust Memorial Museum: Aryan](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/aryan-1).

**Recommendation:** Hello Aryan remains viable for an India-first sprint if the résumé project can be retired. For a long-term international brand, explore AskOutside and further alternatives before committing. D17 stays recorded until you agree to replace it.

## Logo and label practices

A **wordmark** is the name drawn as the logo. A **symbol** is the standalone shape used for an app icon or browser tab. A **descriptor** is the short line explaining the service. We need those digital assets, not a physical packaging label.

- Start with a readable wordmark and one clear visual idea. Avoid trying to draw a phone, robot, human, shop and bridge in the same mark.
- Develop in black first. Then check white-on-dark and a single-colour version; colour should not rescue an unclear shape.
- Check the symbol at 16, 24 and 32 pixels and the wordmark at actual mobile-header size. A small symbol may need a simpler drawing than the full logo.
- Define empty space around the logo and a minimum size based on its own geometry. IBM's published system illustrates these rules; its exact measurements should not be copied. [IBM logo guidance](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/ibm-logos/8-bar/).
- Compare against nearby competitors so it is recognisable as ours. Check unintended shapes as well as spelling.
- Deliver SVG, a vector format that stays sharp at different sizes, plus transparent PNG exports, a browser icon and a one-page usage guide. Record font licences before using a font in the finished mark.
- Keep the service description readable and separate from the symbol. Do not cram the tagline into the small app icon.

**Proposed direction for this project:** a calm black wordmark, with a simple connection or passage motif explored as a separate symbol. Avoid generic AI sparkles, robot faces, headset agents and green approval ticks. Green already means confirmed stock in DESIGN.md; using it as a blanket brand signal would muddy that meaning.

The approved chat cards remain logo-free under D65/D77 and DESIGN.md. A website logo and app listing icon can exist without putting branding inside every result card. No screen or design rule changed in this work.
