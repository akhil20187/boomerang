# Scenario briefs: worked examples

Companion to [the task intake design](2026-10-09-task-intake-and-call-brief-design.md). Each example shows the intake questions asked, the answers, the filled five-field brief and the in-call rules the scenario taught. Drafts from a brainstorming session; no provider selected, no call placed.

## 1. House painter, Kondapur (9 October 2026)

**Customer request:** "I'm looking for a painter who can help paint my home."

### Intake questions and answers

| Step | Question (with choices) | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Availability only, rough price too, or arrange a visit? | Who does it, plus a rough price |
| Recipient | Whole interior, interior plus exterior, or one or two rooms? | One or two rooms |
| Recipient | Area? | Kondapur |
| Recipient | Repaint in normal condition, or cracks and dampness needing putty? | Repaint over existing paint, normal condition |
| Recipient | Paint preference, or painter suggests? | Customer buys paint later; labour cost only |
| Recipient | When? | No fixed date |
| Recipient | Room sizes (for example 10 x 12 ft)? | Unknown. Fallback: ask the per square foot rate |
| Follow-up | If the painter needs a visit before quoting? | Ask the per square foot labour rate as a ballpark and whether the site visit is free |

Room size was added during the session: painters quote per square foot of wall, so size turns a rate into an estimate.

### Brief

1. **Task context:** Kondapur home, one or two rooms, repaint over existing paint in normal condition. Customer buys the paint separately. Room sizes unknown. No fixed date.
2. **Call purpose:** confirm they do house painting in Kondapur and get a rough labour-only cost.
3. **Possible expected outcome:** a ballpark labour rate (per square foot or per room), whether a site visit is free, and roughly how long the work takes. If they will not quote without a visit, record that plus the rate basis they use.
4. **Who the agent represents:** the customer, first name only. No number shared.
5. **Call recipient details:** the selected painter's saved name and number.

### In-call rules this scenario taught

- Say "labour only, we will buy the paint" before asking price, or the number includes material.
- When a number comes back, confirm the unit: per square foot, per room, or total.
- Ask "do you work in Kondapur" before price.
- No booking, no visit scheduled, no promise of when the customer will call back.

### Example call

1. "Hello, is this [painter name]?"
2. "Hi, this is Gayatri. I'm calling for a customer in Kondapur who wants one or two rooms repainted. Do you take that kind of work?"
3. If yes: "Do you work in Kondapur?"
4. "It's a repaint over existing paint, and the customer will buy the paint themselves. What's your labour rate, roughly per square foot?"
5. If "need to see it first": "Understood. Is the site visit free?" then "What rate do you usually charge per square foot for labour?"
6. If a number is given: "Is that per square foot, labour only?"
7. "Roughly how many days would one or two rooms take?"
8. "Thanks. So that's about [rate] per square foot labour only, and a [free / paid] visit. I'll pass that on."

Pause after each question. Skip anything already answered.

## 2. Notary, Balanagar (9 October 2026)

**Customer request:** "I'm looking for a notary service provider in Balanagar who can help me notarize my rental lease agreement."

### Intake questions and answers

| Step | Question (with choices) | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Open and walk in, plus fee, or plus what to bring? | Whether they do it and are open for a walk-in, or whether they want the document sent by SMS/WhatsApp so they can keep it ready |
| Recipient | When? | Today possible, or in a couple of days |
| Recipient | Already printed, draft needing stamp paper, or needs drafting? | Draft ready; needs stamp paper and notarization |
| Recipient | Who will sign: customer alone, both parties, or ask? | Customer goes alone; landlord signs separately later |

Area and document type came from the request, so they were not re-asked.

### Brief

1. **Task context:** Rental lease draft is ready. Needs stamp paper and notarization. Customer goes alone; landlord signs separately. Visit today or in a couple of days.
2. **Call purpose:** confirm they notarize rental agreements in Balanagar, provide stamp paper, are open for a walk-in, and whether one party can come alone.
3. **Possible expected outcome:** yes or no on each of the above, walk-in hours, and whether they want the draft in advance. If yes, get the notary's WhatsApp number so the customer can send it. Fee only if volunteered.
4. **Who the agent represents:** the customer, first name only. No number shared.
5. **Call recipient details:** the selected notary's saved name and number.

### In-call rules this scenario taught

- Ask "open" and "notary present" separately.
- Ask "stamp paper available" and "notarize" separately.
- Ask the one-party question before closing; it decides whether the visit is useful.
- Never offer the customer's number. If they want the document in advance, ask for their WhatsApp number; the customer sends it.
- Share the minimum. Do not volunteer the landlord arrangement, the reason for the lease or any other detail unless the notary asks or it changes the answer.

### Example call

1. "Hello, is this [notary name]?"
2. "Hi, this is Gayatri. I'm calling for a customer in Balanagar. Do you notarize rental agreements?"
3. If yes: "Do you provide the stamp paper as well?"
4. "Is the notary available today for a walk-in?" If not today: "What about the next couple of days? What hours?"
5. "The customer has the draft ready. Can one party come alone, with the landlord signing separately?"
6. If they want the draft in advance: "Which WhatsApp number should the customer send it to?"
7. "Thanks. So [walk-in hours], stamp paper [yes/no], one party alone [yes/no]. I'll pass that on."

Pause after each question. Skip anything already answered. Do not share the customer's number or promise a visit time.

## 3. Tailor, Chintal (9 October 2026)

**Customer request:** "I am looking to a tailor for my saree."

### Intake questions and answers

| Step | Question (with choices) | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Blouse stitching, fall and pico, or alteration? | Blouse stitching |
| Outcome | Open to drop in; plus price and turnaround; plus specific design? | All three |
| Recipient | Area? | Chintal |
| Recipient | Plain, lining or padding, or designer work? | Lining or padding |
| Recipient | Visit for measurements, give an old blouse, or not decided? | Not decided; agent asks what they accept |
| Recipient | Needed by a date? | Thursday 15 October 2026 |

The first question narrowed "tailor for my saree" to the actual job before anything else; the outcome question only makes sense once the job is known.

### Brief

1. **Task context:** Saree blouse stitching with lining or padding, in Chintal. Needed by Thursday 15 October. Measurement method not decided (visit or sample blouse). Blouse fabric assumed to be the saree's blouse piece; say so only if asked.
2. **Call purpose:** confirm they stitch blouses with lining or padding, are open for a drop-in, and can deliver by Thursday.
3. **Possible expected outcome:** yes or no on lining/padding, drop-in hours, approximate price for a lined or padded blouse, normal turnaround, whether Thursday is possible, and whether they take measurements in person or copy an old blouse. Any rush charge if mentioned.
4. **Who the agent represents:** the customer, first name only.
5. **Call recipient details:** the selected tailor's saved name and number.

### In-call rules this scenario taught

- Say "with lining or padding" before asking price, or the answer is the plain-blouse price.
- Ask normal turnaround first, then "can it be ready by Thursday?" as the deciding question. Pair it with a drop-off assumption ("if dropped tomorrow") so the yes means something.
- Confirm whether the price is per blouse and includes lining material.
- Do not mention the saree, the occasion or the reason for Thursday.

### Example call

1. "Hello, is this [tailor name]?"
2. "Hi, this is Gayatri. I'm calling for a customer in Chintal. Do you stitch saree blouses with lining or padding?"
3. If yes: "Roughly what do you charge for a lined or padded blouse?" then if unclear: "Is that per blouse, including the lining material?"
4. "How many days do you normally take?"
5. "If the customer drops it tomorrow, can it be ready by Thursday?"
6. "Do you take measurements in person, or can the customer give an old blouse to copy?"
7. "What time can the customer come by?"
8. "Thanks. So about [price], ready by Thursday if dropped tomorrow, [measurement method]. I'll pass that on."

Pause after each question. Skip anything already answered.

## Rules collected across scenarios

- Share only what changes the recipient's answer; everything else only if asked.
- State conditions before price; confirm units after.
- Split compound facts (open vs notary present; stamp paper vs notarization; customization vs delivery).
- Ask the deciding question before closing (one party alone; site visit free).
- For anything to be sent, get the recipient's channel; the customer sends.
- Narrow a vague request to the actual job before asking about outcome (tailor: blouse vs fall vs alteration).
- A deadline question needs its start assumption stated ("if dropped tomorrow").
